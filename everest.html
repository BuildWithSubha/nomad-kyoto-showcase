MATHEMATICAL TRIUMPH & HERITAGE

WHEN INDIA CALCULATED THE ROOF OF THE WORLD

Long before human boots ever stood atop its summit, Mount Everest’s identity as Earth’s highest point was unlocked through the mathematical genius of an Indian prodigy in Dehradun.

Radhanath Sickdhar & Peak XV (1852)

In 1852, working from the headquarters of the Great Trigonometrical Survey in Dehradun and Calcutta, Indian mathematician and Chief Computer Radhanath Sickdhar computed the raw trigonometric data collected by theodolites.

Correcting for atmospheric refraction and spherical geometry across 240 kilometers of distance, Sickdhar burst into the Surveyor General’s office announcing: "Sir, I have discovered the highest mountain in the world!" Peak XV was measured at exactly 29,000 feet.

Following the 1953 historic first ascent, Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru established the Himalayan Mountaineering Institute (HMI) in Darjeeling with Tenzing Norgay as Director of Field Training, birthing India's modern alpine legacy.

Captain M.S. Kohli

Leader of the monumental 1965 Indian Mount Everest Expedition which placed an unprecedented 9 climbers on the summit of Everest — a world record that stood unbroken for 17 years. This expedition ignited modern mountaineering across India.

SANCTUARY BENEATH THE STARS

NIGHT ON THE KHUMBU GLACIER

At 5,364 meters, the air is so crisp and pure that the Milky Way appears close enough to touch. Inside the glowing geodesic tents, climbers review weather charts, drink warm ginger tea, and listen to the ancient grinding pulse of the glacier beneath them.

Before any expedition departs through the dangerous Khumbu Icefall, the Sherpa Lamas perform the Puja, stringing hundreds of colorful prayer flags into the sky to ask the mountain deity for safe passage and return.
